Why ordinary search isn’t enough

You simply don’t see some of the jobs that fit

A blind spot

Large employers publish jobs in their own recruiting systems — Workday, SuccessFactors and others; smaller ones only on the state portal. Many never reach the big job board.

You can’t cover it by hand

To avoid missing them you’d have to know hundreds of companies, find where each one posts and check them all regularly. That’s hard to keep up for long.

A cluttered feed

The same job appears on the portal, on the board and at the employer; agency ads and stale listings mix in. It’s hard to tell what is new and what is one job.

Everything is scattered

Applications, replies and interviews are spread across spreadsheets and bookmarks. Pulling it together is a job in itself.

What roleorienta does

Four things the big
job boards do poorly

roleorienta works in depth: it goes from the company register to each employer’s own channels — and brings back one clean list.

Works without you

New jobs every hour

The app reads the state portal, the job board and every connected recruiting system on its own — politely, within each site’s limits and robots.txt — and adds new matching jobs to your list every hour.

  • Your conditions are saved: change them, and the list is rebuilt from everything already collected.
  • “New since last visit” shows only what arrived while you were away.
  • An email digest with a frequency you choose is built and switches on with the server stage.

One job, one record

The same job never
shows up three times

A job published on the portal, on the board and in the employer’s system is one entry in your results — and it closes only when it is gone from every channel.

  • A source that is down or read only partly leaves its jobs open.
  • A job closes after it is missing from several complete reads in a row.
  • Jobs the employer published more than 4 months ago step aside.

Application tracking

From application to
outcome — nothing gets lost

Mark a job as applied, move it to interview or employer declined — every change is kept in the job’s history, and your applications sort themselves by state.

  • Active applications on top; silent ones after 3 weeks; completed ones below.
  • A job closed by the employer stays in your applications with a mark.
  • Dismissed jobs wait in “Not interested” and can be returned.

Good to know

Questions that matter

Do you promise every job on the market?

No. Coverage grows with the sources and formats the app can read, and it is honest about the rest. The administrator sees which companies lose jobs and closes the gaps one by one.

Where does the data come from and how current is it?

From the state job portal, the job board and employers’ own recruiting systems and career pages. Sources are read at least once a day, the portal hourly; your results are updated every hour.

Why don’t I see the source and the last check date?

By design: what matters to you is getting the job in time, not which channel it came through — often it came through several. You see when the job was opened and whether the employer closed it, and the link takes you to the original posting.

How does the app know a job is my profession?

From the employer’s code on the state portal, the job board’s position, the position dictionary, or the similarity of the title to titles with a known profession. If none of them is sure, the job isn’t guessed into your list.

Can I add a company the app missed?

Yes. Find it in Companies and add the address of its career page: the app checks it and starts reading it.

Do you send applications for me?

No. You always apply yourself; the app only keeps track of what you did.

Are my applications private?

Yes. One user can’t see another’s conditions, lists or marks, and collection works without any user data.
